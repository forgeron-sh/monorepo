# Customer story

What a customer changed with us, in their words, with their results. Only a real customer, and only what they approved.

## Structure

1. **Headline**: the customer and the outcome.
2. **Results first**: up to three figures, each with what it counts, from the customer.
3. **The company**: who they are and at what scale, in two or three sentences.
4. **Before**: what they used, what it cost, what did not work.
5. **What they built**: the tools, in the order they shipped, who uses them and how.
6. **The results**, explained: where each figure comes from.
7. **What comes next.**
8. **Facts**: industry, size, location, what they use.

Quotes sit beside the claims they support, not in a block at the end.

## Title

"How <customer> <verb> <outcome>", or the outcome as a claim: "Three suite modules replaced by three light tools".

## Opening

Who the customer is, at what scale, and the result.

## Evidence

Every figure comes from the customer, with its period. Quotes are one or two sentences, named, with the role. Every product named is named exactly. No adjective without a number behind it.

## Approval

The customer approves, in writing, every quote, every figure, the logo and the final text. Until then the piece stays a draft, and no invented placeholder is published.

## Length

600 to 1,500 words.

## Checklist

- [ ] The customer approved quotes, figures, logo and text in writing.
- [ ] The headline names the customer and the outcome.
- [ ] Every figure has its source and period.
- [ ] Quotes are short, named, with roles, and placed beside their claims.
- [ ] Products are named exactly.
- [ ] Before, what they built and results are all concrete.
- [ ] One call to action at most.

Drawn from: Stripe, Vercel, Cloudflare and Anthropic customer pages (see `registry.json`). Customer approval is standard practice; no published guide was found for it.
