# Blog post

A product or company story: a launch, a decision, a lesson, an announcement. It earns its place with at least one of: a decision and its trade-offs, original data, a real debugging story, an honest failure, or a how-to that saves real time. Without one of these, it is a changelog entry.

## Structure

Built on the reader's questions, in their order:

1. **What problem does it solve?** One or two paragraphs, concrete, with who has the problem.
2. **How does it work?** Most of the post. Show it: a screenshot, a diagram, a short code sample.
3. **What did it cost?** The trade-offs, the alternatives rejected and why.
4. **How do I use it?** The steps, the command, the link.

## Title

A specific claim or outcome; "Name: what it does" for a launch. Never "Introducing X" alone.

## Opening

The problem or the conclusion in the first two or three sentences.

## Evidence

Every claim of speed, cost or scale carries a number with its baseline. Every screenshot shows the real product. Every code sample runs.

## Length

800 to 1,500 words. Shorter when the material is short.

## Checklist

- [ ] It passes the share test: a decision, data, a debugging story, a failure, or a how-to.
- [ ] The point is in the first three sentences.
- [ ] The headings alone tell the story.
- [ ] Every claim has a number or a source; no `[NEED]` left unresolved.
- [ ] No hype word; no tell of generated prose.
- [ ] It ends with a way to try it, the docs or the source.
- [ ] It says something the changelog does not.
- [ ] Summary of 160 characters or fewer.
