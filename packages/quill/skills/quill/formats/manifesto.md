# Manifesto, essay or position

What we believe and why: a manifesto, an essay, the public version of a white paper. One idea, argued in a line, as short as the idea allows.

## Structure

1. **The idea in one sentence**, first.
2. **The problem in the world** that makes it true now, with concrete examples.
3. **The convictions**, each a heading that is a claim, each argued with an example, not a slogan.
4. **What follows**: what we build, and what we will not do.
5. **The end, when the argument ends.** No recap.

Digressions go to footnotes or out.

## Title

A short claim, or the plain name of the piece ("The Forgeron manifesto").

## Opening

The most important sentence first.

## Evidence

Specific examples over slogans; a position others hold is stated fairly before it is answered. Grand claims become precise ones or go.

## Length

As short as the idea allows: 1,000 to 2,500 words for a manifesto.

## Checklist

- [ ] The idea fits in one sentence, and that sentence is in the first paragraph.
- [ ] Every conviction has a concrete example.
- [ ] Read aloud, every sentence sounds like a person talking.
- [ ] No word chosen to sound impressive.
- [ ] Every ambiguous phrase rewritten: it will be read in the way that does the most harm.
- [ ] It says something a reader did not know or had not put into words.
- [ ] Two readers outside the team flagged what was dull or unclear, and it was fixed.
- [ ] It ends when the argument ends.
