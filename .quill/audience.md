# Forgeron: who we write for

Name the reader before the outline. A piece written for two readers is two pieces.

| Reader                 | What they want from us                                                    | What they already know                      | Where they read                     |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| The internal developer | A simple stack, a CLI that does everything, tests, one-command deployment | TypeScript, Git, an API, roughly MCP        | Blog (engineering), changelog, docs |
| The admin or IT lead   | Who accesses what, SSO once, revoking in one click, an audit trail        | SSO, permissions, compliance vocabulary     | Blog (product), customer stories    |
| The decision maker     | The cost of a suite against one subscription and tools that fit           | Their suite's bill, not the stack           | Manifesto, customer stories         |
| The builder community  | How we built a piece of the lab, with numbers and code                    | Graphics, editors, compilers, the web stack | Lab papers, engineering posts       |

The business user (sales, support, management) never sees Forgeron: they talk to Claude. Write about them, not to them.

Source: `docs/white-paper.md`, Who it's for.
